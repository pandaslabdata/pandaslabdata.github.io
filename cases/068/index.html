What if the Moon disappeared tonight?

You'd be fine, and Earth stays on course. But the tides, the nights and, slowly, the seasons would change. Here is the evidence, and where the science still disagrees.

01 · The Question

One night, the sky is empty

The Moon has 1.23% of Earth's mass OBSERVED. It looks small, so would anyone notice if it vanished?

Earth would keep orbiting the Sun. The Earth–Moon balance point sits about 4,670 km from Earth's center, inside the planet itself SCENARIO. Nothing flies off course. The changes are quieter, and some of them take millions of years.

03 · The Simulation

The slow suspect: Earth's tilt

Today Earth's tilt swings between 22.1° and 24.5° over about 41,000 years OBSERVED. The Moon helps keep it steady. Without it, two published simulations disagree on how far it could drift.

04 · The Verdict

Not the end of the world. Still open.

What we know: Earth stays in orbit, the tides shrink to the Sun's share, nights get much darker, and total solar eclipses disappear.

What we don't: how far the tilt would really drift. Later work suggests the drift is slow and should not be read as a sign of extreme climate swings HYPOTHESIS. Losing the Moon wouldn't end the world. It might shake up the seasons.

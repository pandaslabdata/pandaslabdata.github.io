01 · The Question

Does the dinosaur map show where dinosaurs lived, or where we managed to find them?

In our archive of 3,640 records drawn from the Paleobiology Database, three countries hold 59% of all finds and 73% sit between 30° and 60° north. Canada and China have similar numbers of finds, yet China's records contain about four times as many species.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said six dinosaurs were found in Antarctica, under the ice. The data show six named species, from rock exposed at Mount Kirkpatrick or on ice-free ground in the James Ross Island area, not from beneath the ice.
  • The video said 18 of the 40 largest countries were blank and the entire Congo Basin had zero finds. The data show this is true only for our species-level archive: in the full Paleobiology Database just 5 of the 18 lack any dinosaur record, Sudan has 18 records, Libya 11, Bolivia 140 (mostly footprints) and DR Congo 1.
  • The video said, quoting Kenneth Lacovara, that no other paleontologist was within 1,000 kilometers. The sources show that line could not be verified; in a 2014 Drexel interview he says he chose southern Patagonia because almost all Patagonian species came from the north and center.
  • The video said 225 metric tons of bones were shipped to Berlin. The sources show 225 metric tons is the lowest estimate, with others reaching 250, and the shipment was fossils with their surrounding rock, not bones alone.
  • The video said Canada's 537 finds and China's 546 were practically identical. The data show that holds in our curated archive, while the raw Paleobiology Database has 778 and 912; the species contrast (135 versus 558 there) still holds.
  • The video said ten grid squares hold almost half of all finds. The data show 47% in our archive and 42% in the raw Paleobiology Database, and the share depends on where the 5° grid is placed.
05 · The Verdict

The episode's central point holds: in both our archive and the full Paleobiology Database, finds cluster in a few countries and in the northern mid-latitudes, and equal piles of records can hide very different numbers of species. The patterns track where suitable rock is exposed and where people have searched, though blank countries are often 'not identified to species' rather than truly empty. A few lines overstated the case: the Antarctic fossils came from exposed rock, the Tendaguru tonnage is an estimate, and the Lacovara quote could not be verified.

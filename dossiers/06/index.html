01 · The Question

Why are two out of three cataloged meteorites found in Antarctica, if space isn't aiming there?

In NASA's Meteorite Landings data, 68.1% of 45,716 meteorites recorded through 2013 were found in Antarctica, yet none was seen falling. Witnessed falls held steady for a century while finds exploded after 1969: the map shows where ice and deserts preserve stones and people search.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video's title said 68% of all meteorites are found in one place. The data show 68.1% of the 45,716 meteorites cataloged through 2013 in NASA's Meteorite Landings dataset, many of them fragments of the same fall; today the share is just over 60% of about 80,000 finds.
  • The video said Antarctica yielded about 5,400, 7,600, 11,000 and 15,640 finds in the 1970s, 1980s, 1990s and 2000s. The data in the cited catalog show 4,621, 6,365, 8,925 and 10,720.
  • The video said about 17,000 meteorites reach Earth every year. The data show an estimated 17,600 falls a year (range 12,200–25,400), counting only falls heavier than 50 grams, not individual fragments.
  • The video said Japanese researchers found nine meteorites on the ice in 1969, in ten days. The data show the nine meteorites on a blue ice area, but no source we read supports the ten days.
  • The video said the ice works like a conveyor belt that delivers everything to the same drawer. The data show that most meteorites carried within the ice end up in the ocean; only a small fraction resurfaces in blue ice areas.
  • The video said Antarctica keeps meteorites cold, dry, with no rust. The data show that cold, dry conditions slow weathering but do not stop it.
  • The video said a small bump in the 1930s came from private collectors starting to search. The data do not show that cause, so it remains an unverified hypothesis.
05 · The Verdict

The headline holds only with its qualifier: 68.1% of meteorites cataloged through 2013 (NASA's Meteorite Landings dataset) come from Antarctica, and today the share is just over 60% of about 80,000 finds. The core argument checks out in the data: witnessed falls stay flat for a century and avoid Antarctica entirely, while finds explode where ice and deserts preserve stones and people go searching, and fireball data even show fewer falls per km² near the poles. A few figures from the video are updated here against the cited catalog, and none of this changes its conclusion.

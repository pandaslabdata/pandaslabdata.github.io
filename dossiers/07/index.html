01 · The Question

How strong can an earthquake get, and what sets the limit?

In the ISC-GEM catalog of 74,159 earthquakes (1904–2021), Valdivia, Chile, 1960 leads at magnitude 9.55 ± 0.25 and alone holds 22.1% of the computed energy. The ceiling is set by fault length: no known fault is long enough for a magnitude 10.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said the May 22, 1960 earthquakes struck at 10:30 in the morning, then at 6:56 and 7:11 in the evening. The data show these are UTC times; in Chile (UTC−4) it was 06:30, 14:56 and 15:11 local time.
  • The video said Valdivia was a rupture of roughly 1,600 km. Seismological studies estimate the rupture at about 800–1,000 km (920 ± 100 km), while USGS describes the fault as almost 1,000 miles long.
  • The video said Kamchatka 2025 lands fifth or sixth, depending on the catalog. The data show sixth in both: below Kamchatka 1952 (Mw 8.84) in ISC-GEM, and tied for sixth in the USGS ranking.
  • The video said Valdivia released a fifth of all the energy measured since 1904. The data show 22.1% of the energy calculated from the magnitudes of the 74,159 cataloged earthquakes (1904–2021), which leave out smaller events.
05 · The Verdict

The answer holds: the strongest earthquake recorded by instruments is Valdivia, Chile, 1960, at Mw 9.55 ± 0.25 in ISC-GEM, and the catalog numbers in the video (ranking, energy units, 22.1%, 92 events, decade counts) reproduce exactly from the data file. The ceiling argument matches USGS: magnitude grows with fault length, and no known fault is long enough for a magnitude 10. The Update section adds the local times of May 22, 1960, the estimated rupture length, the 2025 ranking and the scope of the 22.1%.

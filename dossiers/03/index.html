01 · The Question

Was T. rex really the biggest meat-eating dinosaur, or just the most famous one?

By published length estimates, T. rex ranks fourth of four giant predators, though the margins are small and every figure is an estimate. What it leads is history: named in 1905, 90 years before Giganotosaurus, it appears 63 times in our archive against 3.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said all three rivals lived between 99 and 94 million years ago, about 30 million years before T. rex. The data show this holds for Spinosaurus and Carcharodontosaurus, but Giganotosaurus probably dates to 113-106 million years ago, so the gap is at least 26 million years, and more than 40 for Giganotosaurus.
  • The video said Spinosaurus was 15 meters long, as a single figure. The data show that is the 2014 estimate; a 2022 reconstruction gives just under 14 meters, still the longest of the four.
  • The video gave Giganotosaurus a precise length above 13 meters. The data show that figure is extrapolated from a single jaw fragment; the type specimen was estimated at 12.5 meters, nearly level with Sue's 12.3 meters, so its lead over T. rex is within the uncertainty.
  • The video gave a median length of 3.4 meters right after counting 1,050 finds. The data show 3.4 meters is the median across 310 genera; counted per find, the median is 3.5 meters.
05 · The Verdict

By published length estimates, T. rex is not the longest meat-eating dinosaur: Spinosaurus clearly is, while Giganotosaurus and Carcharodontosaurus edge it by less than a meter, within the uncertainty of the estimates. None of this crowns a new 'biggest ever,' because the rivals are known from partial or lost skeletons. What T. rex truly leads is history: named first, in 1905, and found far more often, which is a crown for fame, not size.

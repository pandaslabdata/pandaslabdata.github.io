01 · The Question

How deep is the Mariana Trench, and how well do we really know its deepest point?

Challenger Deep lies about 10,935 m below sea level, so an upside-down Everest would stop about 2 km short of the bottom. At true scale the trench is a broad, gentle depression, and since 2020 more people have reached it than have walked on the Moon.

02 · The Evidence
Swipe the evidence→
OBSERVED

The best current estimate puts Challenger Deep at 10,935 m below mean sea level, ± 6 m at 95% confidence, from pressure-based measurements taken by a crewed submersible in June 2020.

C2 · Greenaway et al. 2021, Deep-Sea Research Part I 178 (NOAA Repository)
ESTIMATE

At true scale the trench is a broad depression. In the GEBCO_2020 grid, a cross-section near Challenger Deep has walls averaging about 5–10° (locally steeper, up to about 18° over 2 km) and is about 11–13 km wide at 10,000 m below sea level.

C5 · GEBCO_2020 Grid, 15 arc-second (NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP)
ESTIMATE

Very little seafloor lies deeper than 10,000 m. In the GEBCO_2020 grid that zone covers about 1,480 km² in the Mariana Trench and about 975 km² in the Tonga Trench, less than Greater London (about 1,572 km²) and under 2% of Portugal's 92,230 km².

C8 · GEBCO_2020 Grid, 15 arc-second (NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP)
OBSERVED

On June 26, 2020, Jim Wigginton became the 13th person to reach Challenger Deep. Only 12 people have walked on the Moon, so more people had now been to the deepest point of the ocean.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said the trench is about 16 km wide at 10,000 m below sea level. In the GEBCO_2020 grid, a cross-section perpendicular to the trench axis near Challenger Deep measures about 11–13 km at that depth.
  • The video said a vehicle like Deepsea Challenger takes around 2 hours to reach the bottom. The data show that its 2012 descent took 2 hours and 36 minutes.
05 · The Verdict

The headline holds: the best current estimate puts Challenger Deep at 10,935 m ± 6 m, about 2 km deeper than Everest is tall. The data support the episode's main points: at true scale the trench is a broad depression, modern estimates still differ by meters to tens of meters, Horizon Deep in the Tonga Trench comes within about 1%, and since June 2020 more people have reached the bottom than have walked on the Moon. The trench width at 10,000 m and Deepsea Challenger's descent time differ from the values in the video, as noted in the Update.

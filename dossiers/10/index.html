01 · The Question

How far from land is the ocean's most remote point, and who would be your nearest neighbors there?

Point Nemo, the ocean point farthest from land, sits about 2,688 km from three islands in the published record, or 2,704.5 km on a coastline that omits tiny Maher Island. There, astronauts on the ISS, 370–460 km up, can be closer than anyone ashore.

02 · The Evidence
Swipe the evidence→
ESTIMATE

Recomputed on the GSHHG full-resolution coastline (v2.3.7, 2017), the farthest point from land sits at about 49°01.7'S 123°25.6'W, 2,704.5 km from Motu Nui, Ducie Island and the Antarctic coast near Siple Island, 17 km from the published point.

C2 · Wessel & Smith, GSHHG v2.3.7 full-resolution coastline (SOEST, University of Hawaii; LGPL)
OBSERVED

Maher Island, about 1 km long, is missing from the GSHHG coastline. Measured to Maher's listed position, the published point sits 2,688 km from all three islands, so most of the 16 km gap comes from what each map includes.

ESTIMATE

With the same method and dataset, the Atlantic's most remote point (24.2°N 43.4°W) is 2,034 km from land and the Indian Ocean's (47.6°S 100.0°E) is 1,939 km. The Pacific's 2,704.5 km is the largest.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said the original 1992 point lies 2,691 km from land in the modern dataset, about 13 km short of the new maximum. The data show 2,688 km on the same WGS84 ellipsoid used for the 2,704.5 km maximum (2,691 km is the value on a sphere), a gap of about 16 km.
  • The video said the 22.6 million km² circle around the recomputed point holds no mapped island. The data show Maher Island, absent from the GSHHG coastline but listed by NOAA, about 2,671 km from that point, inside the circle.
05 · The Verdict

Point Nemo's published position, about 2,688 km from Ducie, Motu Nui and Maher islands, holds up, and the channel's 2,704.5 km recalculation reproduces on the GSHHG coastline, where the difference comes mostly from Maher Island being absent. Two other ocean poles (2,034 km and 1,939 km) confirm the Pacific's lead, and simple geometry shows that ISS astronauts, 370–460 km up, can be the nearest humans whenever the station is above the horizon and no ship is closer. Mir's 2001 re-entry east of New Zealand and NASA's plan for a remote-ocean re-entry of the ISS after 2030 are documented, while spacecraft cemetery counts remain press estimates.

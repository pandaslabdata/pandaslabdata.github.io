04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said our dataset had 15 Stegosaurus finds. The data show 84 Late Jurassic occurrences in the Paleobiology Database (82 in the US, 2 in Portugal); 15 is only the subset identified to species.
  • The video gave 12.3 meters and about 9 tonnes as the size of T. rex in general. The data show these are the figures for SUE, the largest known specimen, while most adults are estimated at 6 to 8 tonnes.
  • The video said Stegosaurus weighed about 5 tonnes, without saying this was an estimate. The data show published estimates from about 1.6 to roughly 7 tonnes, depending on method and specimen; 5 falls inside that range.
  • The video said no species appears in both worlds. The data show one species in both intervals, Richardoestesia gilmorei, a name based on isolated teeth, so the zero holds only for valid species, a condition the video did not state aloud.
  • The video counted 168 species in the world of Stegosaurus and 290 in that of T. rex, from an internal dataset that cannot be reproduced. The data show 147 and 356 in a documented Paleobiology Database query, and the conclusion does not change.
  • The video said our dataset had 63 T. rex finds across the United States, Canada and Mexico. The data show 68 occurrences today (63 in the US, 4 in Canada, 1 in Mexico), and the Mexican record is a single set of isolated teeth.
05 · The Verdict

We know that Stegosaurus and T. rex never met: they are separated by at least 77 million years, more than the 66 million years between the last T. rex and us, and no fossil site or valid species links their two worlds. Their dates come from dated rock layers and international timescales, so the size of the gap is solid even though each boundary carries uncertainty of up to a few million years. What remains uncertain is how big these animals were: body-mass estimates depend on method and specimen, ranging from about 1.6 to 7 tonnes for Stegosaurus and 6 to 9.5 tonnes for adult T. rex.

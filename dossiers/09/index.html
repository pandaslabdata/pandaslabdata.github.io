How big is the universe? At least 93 billion light-years across

A ladder of light-travel times climbs from the Moon (1.28 light-seconds) and Voyager 1 (23.8 light-hours) to an observable universe about 93 billion light-years wide. That width exceeds 13.8 billion years of light travel because space kept expanding while the light was on its way.

01 · The Question

If the universe is 13.8 billion years old, how can the part we see be about 93 billion light-years wide?

A ladder of light-travel times climbs from the Moon (1.28 light-seconds) and Voyager 1 (23.8 light-hours) to an observable universe about 93 billion light-years wide. That width exceeds 13.8 billion years of light travel because space kept expanding while the light was on its way.

The observable universe is estimated at about 93 billion light-years across today; our recomputation with Planck 2018 parameters gives a radius of 46.2 and a diameter of 92.4 billion light-years. It exceeds 13.8 billion light-years because space expanded while the light traveled.

04 · Update

Dossier update, November 2026. We went back over every figure in the episode with the sources and the latest data. Point by point, here is where the numbers stand today.

  • The video said light crosses our planetary backyard in about four hours. The data show that about 4.2 hours is the light travel time from the Sun to Neptune; crossing Neptune's orbit from side to side takes about 8.3 hours.
05 · The Verdict

The ladder holds: each rung, from the Moon at 1.28 light-seconds to Laniakea at about 520 million light-years, matches its source, and Voyager 1 was 172.0 AU from the Sun (23.8 light-hours) in October 2026. The 93 billion light-years is the estimated present-day diameter of the observable universe, not of the whole universe; our Planck 2018 recomputation gives 92.4. The reachable share of about 4% belongs to the Davis & Lineweaver model, and with Planck 2018 values it is 4.6%.
